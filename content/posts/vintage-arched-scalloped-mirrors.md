A mirror is the décor multitasker — it adds character *and* bounces light around a small cottage room. An arched or scalloped frame does it with vintage charm. Here are the ones worth hanging.

**Quick picks:** Best scallop — Viona · Best statement — Gezella · Best for entryways — Arendahl · Best black — arched windowpane.

## Arched vs. scalloped vs. windowpane

**Arched** mirrors feel soft and classic; **scalloped** frames add a playful, decorative edge; **windowpane** mirrors mimic an old cottage window and read more rustic. Match the finish to your room — warm gold for soft cottage, black for moody farmhouse.

## What to look for before you buy

Six things decide whether a mirror looks like something you inherited or like a large shiny disc screwed to the wall.

**Frame depth.** A frame that stands proud of the glass by half an inch or more throws its own shadow line and reads as furniture. Flat, printed-looking frames read as a product photo no matter how good the shape is.

**Glass flatness.** Cheap float glass in a large mirror ripples. In the listing photos, find a straight line in the reflection — a door frame, a skirting board — and follow it. Any wobble visible on screen will be worse on your wall.

**The hanging hardware, or the absence of it.** Anything over about 15 lb should arrive with D-rings and wire, not a single sawtooth hanger. If the listing does not say which, assume sawtooth and budget for a proper set.

**Weight against your actual wall.** Plaster and lath takes a heavy mirror badly. Over 20 lb wants a stud or a real toggle bolt, and a 42-inch windowpane is over 20 lb more often than not.

**Scallop count.** Fewer, larger scallops — roughly twelve to sixteen around a 26-inch round — read vintage. Many small ones read novelty. They are countable in the photograph, so count them.

**Finish rather than colour.** Antique gold, brushed brass and aged black all have some grey in them. A pure bright gold or a flat dead black is the tell that a frame is new and trying not to look it.

## Size it to the wall, not the table

A common mistake is a mirror that's too small. Aim for a mirror roughly two-thirds the width of the console or mantel beneath it, hung so the center sits near eye level. Over a mantel, leave four to six inches of clear space above the shelf; over a console, six to eight. In a hallway with no furniture under it at all, put the centre of the glass 57 to 60 inches off the floor — the same height gallery walls are hung at, and where most people's eyeline actually lands.

## Where these mirrors disappoint

Every one of these has a trade-off, and the listings will not tell you about them.

- **A windowpane mirror is eight small mirrors, not one.** The grid bars mean you never get a clean full-length reflection. Treat it as wall decoration that happens to reflect, not as a mirror you will use.
- **Wooden scalloped frames chip at the points.** The scallop tips are the thinnest part of the frame and the first thing a courier finds. Unpack over a bed, not over a tiled floor.
- **Painted metal frames show every fingerprint.** Black powder-coat especially — each time you nudge it straight you leave marks that only come off with a barely damp cloth.
- **Large arched mirrors are genuinely awkward for one person.** Anything over about 36 inches wants two people and a spirit level, because the wire shifts as you lift and you cannot see the hooks.
- **"Antique gold" drifts between production runs.** If you want two hung as a pair, order them together rather than months apart, or accept that one will be warmer than the other.

## Hanging and care

- Find the stud, or use a toggle bolt rated for at least twice the mirror's weight. A fixing rated exactly to the weight is rated for a static load, and a mirror on a wall next to a door is not a static load.
- Use two hooks rather than one, spaced about a third of the mirror's width apart. It is the single thing that stops a frame drifting crooked every time the door slams.
- Stick felt pads on the bottom two corners. The frame then sits fractionally off the wall, air moves behind it, and the paint does not scuff.
- Clean the glass with a microfibre cloth barely damp with water, and never spray cleaner straight at a framed mirror. Liquid wicks under the frame edge and blackens the silvering, and that damage does not come back.
- Dust a carved or scalloped wooden frame with a soft dry brush rather than a cloth, which snags on the points and pulls at the finish.
- Do not hang a wood-framed mirror directly opposite a sunny window. The finish fades unevenly across the half that catches the light, and it shows within a summer.

## What goes with a scalloped mirror

A scalloped edge wants company, not competition. In a bathroom, [scalloped and waffle towels](/blog/scalloped-waffle-towels) repeat the curve at eye level without adding another hard shape. In a bedroom or hallway, [pleated lampshades](/blog/best-pleated-lampshades) and a [woven wall basket](/blog/cottagecore-woven-wall-baskets) or two give the wall some texture so the mirror is not floating alone, and the wider [grandmillennial decor](/blog/grandmillennial-decor) guide covers how scallops, chintz and brass sit together without tipping into fussy.

Underfoot, a [scalloped floral rug](/blog/scalloped-floral-rugs) picks the curve back up at floor level, which is the trick that makes one scalloped mirror look like a decision rather than an accident. Over a bed, a [ditsy floral quilt](/blog/floral-ditsy-quilts) does the same job in quieter language — small print, no hard edges, nothing on the wall competing for the eye. And in a dark hallway, a black windowpane mirror sits happily beside [vintage botanical wall art](/blog/vintage-botanical-wall-art) in thin black frames.

## FAQ

**Are these heavy to hang?**

The metal-framed windowpane styles are fairly light; the larger arched mirrors need a proper wall anchor. Check each listing's weight and included hardware, and use two hooks rather than one.

**Will a gold frame look too glam for cottagecore?**

An *antique* or brushed gold reads warm and vintage, not flashy — it's the bright, shiny golds to avoid. Hold the listing photo next to a brass doorknob: if the mirror is brighter than the knob, it will look new on your wall.

**What size mirror goes over a 48-inch console?**

Roughly two-thirds the width, so 30 to 34 inches. The 38 x 24 inch Gezella hung portrait is right for a console that size; the 26-inch Viona suits a 36 to 40 inch console better.

**Can I hang a heavy mirror on plasterboard without finding a stud?**

Yes, with snap-toggle or butterfly bolts rated well above the mirror's weight and spread across two fixing points. The plastic push-in anchors that come in the box are not adequate for anything over about 10 lb.

**Do arched mirrors work in a room where everything else is square?**

That is exactly when they work best. The arch becomes the one curve in a room of rectangles, which is why it reads as a deliberate feature rather than as decoration.

**Are scalloped mirrors a trend that will date?**

Scalloped edges have come and gone since the 1930s, so they behave more like a recurring motif than a trend. A wooden or antique-brass scallop will age better than a high-gloss coloured one.

**Should the mirror finish match my door hardware?**

It helps, but it is not a rule. Staying within one metal family — warm with warm, cool with cool — matters far more than matching exactly, and a wooden frame sidesteps the question entirely.

**How do I stop a mirror going crooked?**

Two hooks instead of one, plus a pair of felt or rubber bumpers at the bottom corners. The bumpers add friction against the wall and stop the frame sliding along its wire every time someone walks past.
